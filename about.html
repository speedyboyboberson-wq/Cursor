105 Easton Avenue

A gyro shop, not a concept.

Orange chairs, foil trays, and a window that says eat like a king. That is the whole brand, and it works on this block.

The room

Counter-serve on the Rutgers strip.

King of Gyro sits at 105 Easton Ave, a short walk from the train station and the rest of the late-night corridor. It is a halal kitchen: gyros, chicken, falafel, kofta, fish, burgers, and heroes. You order at the counter. Most tickets come back in a few minutes.

The dining room is bright and simple — orange chairs, a long counter, food photos on the wall, and the crowned smiley in the window. It is not Evelyn’s patio and it is not Stuff Yer Face’s bar. It is the place you go when you want a platter and you want it now.

On this block

Built to sit next to the other Easton Ave kitchens.

Vs. Mamoun’s Falafel

Mamoun’s at 58 Easton is the polished falafel chain, open until 11. King of Gyro stays open three to four hours later and leans platters, white sauce, and gyro meat.

Vs. Evelyn’s

Evelyn’s at 45 Easton is sit-down Lebanese with a patio and reservations. We are the foil-tray alternative: cheaper, faster, and still serving after midnight.

Vs. Stuff Yer Face

Stuff Yer Face is the stromboli-and-beer landmark. When the boli is not the craving, the gyro shop two blocks up is the other Easton Ave constant.

What to order

Rice, sauce, and a tray that actually fills you up.

Regulars talk about the same three things: portion size, the seasoned rice, and the white sauce. The King Platter is three proteins. The mix platter is chicken and lamb. If you want it in your hand, get lamb on a pita.

The kitchen is halal. There is no alcohol. Street parking is the Easton Ave reality; pickup is usually faster than hunting for a table at 1 AM.
